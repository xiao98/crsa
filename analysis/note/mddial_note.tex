\section{Setup and notation}

In the released configuration, the patient is agent $A$ with $\mathcal{M}_A$ the 12 diseases, the
doctor is agent $B$ with a single meaning ($|\mathcal{M}_B| = 1$), and $\mathcal{Y}$ is the set of
12 diseases. The patient chooses between two utterances (yes, no) and the doctor between 118
(one question per symptom). Costs are zero. The modelled turns of a dialog
alternate doctor, patient, \dots, so each dialog has as many doctor turns as patient turns.

The code computes, for dialog $i$ with $T_i$ modelled turns,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PPL} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \exp\Big( -\frac{1}{T_i} \sum_{t=1}^{T_i}
\log S_t\big(u_t^{(i)} \mid w_t^{(i)}, m_{S_t}^{(i)}\big) \Big).
\label{eq:ppl}
\end{equation}
(The formula in Section~6 of the paper has no $1/T_i$; this is only a difference of notation.)

\section{The CRSA speaker is uniform}
\label{sec:uniform}

\paragraph{Observation.} In the released \code{all\_results.pkl}, the CRSA speaker distribution
satisfies $\max_u |S_t(u) - 1/|\mathcal{U}_t|| < 10^{-16}$ on every doctor turn and
$< 3.3 \times 10^{-3}$ on every patient turn. For a uniform speaker, each dialog contributes
\[
\exp\big( \tfrac12 \log 2 + \tfrac12 \log 118 \big) = \sqrt{236} \approx 15.362
\]
to \eqref{eq:ppl}, which is the CRSA entry of Table~1 (bootstrap 95\% interval over dialogs:
$[15.3622, 15.3625]$). The normalised entropy of the belief over the patient's meaning is $1.000$ at every turn.

\paragraph{Doctor turns.} This follows from $|\mathcal{M}_S| = 1$. With a single speaker meaning
$m$, the listener update reads
\[
L^{k+1}(y \mid u, w, m_L) = \frac{B_L(m)\, P(m, m_L, y)\, S^{k+1}(u \mid w, m)}
{\sum_{y'} B_L(m)\, P(m, m_L, y')\, S^{k+1}(u \mid w, m)} = P(y \mid m, m_L),
\]
which does not depend on $u$. The same holds for the literal listener of Eq.~(7), where the
lexicon $\mathcal{L}_{u,w}(m)$ cancels in the normalisation over $y$. The speaker update then has
an exponent that is constant in $u$, so $S(u \mid w, m) = 1/|\mathcal{U}|$. In particular the LLM
scores of the 118 candidate questions do not enter the result.

\paragraph{Patient turns.} Here the uniform solution is not forced, but it is the one reached at
$\alpha = 2.5$. I reimplemented Eqs.~(11)--(13) independently of the repository and ran them to
convergence on the patient turns of 200 random dialogs, with the cached LLM lexicon:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & 2.5 & 5 & 10 & 20 & 40 \\
\midrule
mean of $\max_{a,u} |S(u \mid a) - \tfrac12|$ & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.178 & 0.497 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This looks like the same effect as the remark of Section~5.2 that all iterative algorithms give
uniform distributions for $\alpha \le 1$ in the reference game, with a larger threshold here. A
plausible reason is that the prior couples the target only weakly to the patient's meaning:
$P(y = a \mid m_A = a) = 0.164$ on average. Replacing the LLM lexicon by a synthetic, highly
informative one ($P(\text{yes} \mid a) = 0.95$ if disease $a$ has the symptom and $0.05$ otherwise)
still gives a uniform speaker at $\alpha = 2.5$. With the released CRSA code at $\alpha = 40$ the
speaker is no longer uniform, but the patient-turn perplexity rises from 2.00 to 5.37.

\paragraph{Listener.} The CRSA and RSA listeners predict the same class for all 1703 dialogs. The
task success rate $0.085$ (bootstrap 95\% interval $[0.072, 0.099]$) is the frequency of that
class; chance level is $1/12 = 0.083$.

\section{Normalisation of \texorpdfstring{$P(m_B, y \mid m_A)$}{P(mB, y | mA)} in the RSA speaker}
\label{sec:rsa}

Eq.~(11) uses $P(m_B, y \mid m_A)$. In \code{llm\_models/rsa.py}, \code{Speaker.update} computes

\begin{center}
\code{prior\_by\_given\_a = prior / prior\_a}
\end{center}

with \code{prior} of shape $(|\mathcal{M}_A|, |\mathcal{M}_B|, |\mathcal{Y}|)$ and \code{prior\_a} of
shape $(|\mathcal{M}_A|, 1)$. NumPy aligns trailing axes, so the division is carried out along the
$\mathcal{M}_B$ axis. On MDDial the result has shape $(12, 12, 12)$ and
\[
\sum_{b, y} \texttt{prior\_by\_given\_a}[a, b, y] = P(a) \sum_{a'} \frac{1}{P(a')} =: c_a,
\qquad c_a \in [6.0,\ 22.2],
\]
instead of 1. The exponent of Eq.~(11) is therefore multiplied by $c_a$, which amounts to an
effective rationality $\alpha\, c_a \in [15, 55]$ rather than $2.5$. This is consistent with the
table of Section~\ref{sec:uniform}: the released RSA speaker is the only one that leaves the uniform
solution.

With \code{prior / prior\_a[:, :, np.newaxis]}, the RSA speaker is uniform to within
$3.3 \times 10^{-3}$ and its perplexity is 15.362, identical to CRSA. The CRSA speaker is not
affected on MDDial: the first modelled turn is a doctor turn, which is uniform in any case, and
later turns use $P(y \mid m_A, m_B)$, which is normalised correctly.

\subsection{Reference game}
\label{sec:refgame}

The same line is present in the \code{naive\_models} package: in the RSA and Literal speakers,
and in the first-turn branch of the CRSA speaker. There the prior has shape $(64, 64, 7)$ and $P(m_A)$ ranges
from $0.003$ to $0.029$, so the division is by $P(m_A)$ indexed along the $\mathcal{M}_B$ axis. I
reran the reference game with the same change (game size 6, $\alpha = 2.5$, 500 rounds, same seed).
Utterances are sampled, so the two runs are not comparable round by round.

\section{Normalisation of the Literal speaker}
\label{sec:literal}

Eq.~(8) defines $S^0_t(u \mid m, w) \propto P_{LM}(u \mid w, \mathrm{prompt}(m))$, normalised over
$u$. In \code{LLMLiteral.run\_turn}, the lexicon is normalised jointly,
\[
\mathcal{L}(u, m) = \frac{\exp \ell(u, m)}{\sum_{u', m'} \exp \ell(u', m')},
\]
and the reported speaker distribution is the column $\mathcal{L}(\cdot, m)$. On doctor turns
$|\mathcal{M}_S| = 1$ and the column sums to 1. On patient turns it sums to $0.081$ on average
(about $1/12$), which inflates the perplexity.

After dividing each column by its sum, the Literal perplexity is 16.232. By role, it is 99.64 on
doctor turns (uniform: 118) and 2.424 on patient turns (uniform: 2). So the LLM is informative on
doctor turns and slightly worse than uniform on patient turns.
